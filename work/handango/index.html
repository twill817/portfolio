Handango

I joined as the first employee and built the design function, as the marketplace grew to more than a million monthly visitors and up to $42M in annual sales.

Built a five-person design team and worked across the product, the brand, retail packaging and company strategy.Creative Director, then Senior UX Design Lead · Handango

Problem

Handango sold software for Palm, Pocket PC, Symbian and BlackBerry devices, years before app stores gave anyone a model to follow. The platforms were fragmented and incompatible, buyers had to trust a download and a card payment from a company they didn't know, and many paid for software their phone couldn't actually run.

As a young company, it also needed nearly everything a business needs: a brand, a storefront, the tools to run it, and a clear direction.

Approach

I joined on a short contract as the web designer and stayed on as the company grew around me. Within a year I'd taken on brand and creative direction, and when I was offered a promotion, I asked for the title that matched the work I was already doing. I then grew the design team from just me to five designers in about eighteen months.

We treated the store itself as the product: how the catalog was structured, how people browsed and searched it, and the path from finding something to buying it.

The range

  • Product design. The storefront's structure, catalog browsing and search, and the purchase path, along with the back-office merchandising tools the business ran on.
  • Brand and advertising. The company's identity and advertising, and content design for the software catalog.
  • Packaging. Retail packaging for the microSD memory cards Handango sold through Best Buy's in-store program.
  • Front-end code. I built much of the early site in HTML, CSS and JavaScript myself.
  • Company strategy. When the founder returned as CEO, he asked me directly what Handango should do. I made the case for committing fully to ecommerce over a freeware hybrid, and the company moved in that direction. Separately, as partner deals began, I suggested we white-label our storefront for them, and we did.

Device selection

Compatibility was the biggest problem in the marketplace, for us and for everyone else in it. I spent about a year building the internal case for letting buyers choose their exact device and find only software that would run on it.

Once it was approved, I led design delivery. It went well beyond the front end: the catalog had to model compatibility for each device, not just each operating system, which meant back-end and content-management changes across the whole catalog. No competitor offered anything like it at the time.

Outcome

  • Device selection tripled conversion and cut returns by 60%.
  • I had a direct hand in the site's growth through the UX work, as it reached more than 1.2 million monthly visitors and up to $42M in annual sales.
  • The company adopted both the ecommerce-first direction and the white-label storefront.
  • I'm proud of where the team I built went next, several of them into senior design roles.

My role

As Creative Director, I built and led the design team and owned design across the company. As Handango grew to around 150 people, it brought in a dedicated product and design leadership structure, and I moved into a Senior UX Design Lead role within it. That's where my formal UX practice took shape.
