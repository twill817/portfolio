PA.gov

Sixty-two state sites now share one structure and one search. Agencies could have opted out, and all but a handful didn't.

Associate Creative Director, Experience · Publicis Sapient

Problem

Pennsylvania's services were spread across 62 sites run by 43 agencies. Residents had to know which agency owned their task before they could find it, which was the opposite of the governor's "No wrong door" commitment.

Approach

We worked with each agency to reorganize its site around how residents look for content, not how the agency is set up. Folders gave way to tags, so every page carries its agency, topic, and life events, and search can filter the way residents think.

Early on, we planned to build the experience around life events, but no one on the client side would own that content. So we kept the recommendations simpler and put the effort into examples and guidance agencies could follow on their own.

The work

Outcome

  • PA.gov launched in phases on this architecture, completing in January 2025.
  • The accessibility audit score rose from 37% to 95%.
  • The site had about 9 million visits in December 2024.
  • The tagging and filters are still part of the live search.

Outcome figures are for the full program.

My role

I owned the UX architecture, led four designers, and set direction for the visual design lead, working directly with the governor's digital team. I ran the agency workshops and set the content strategy behind search.
