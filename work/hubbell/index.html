Hubbell

About 40 brand sites, Hubbell.com included, shipped on one shared system with each brand's identity intact.

Brought 30+ people from wary, independent brands behind one system, and wireframed the work.UX Lead · Razorfish, now Publicis Sapient

Problem

Hubbell is a 130-year-old electrical and utility manufacturer made up of subsidiary brands, many of them run as their own businesses with their own P&L and customers. That had produced more than 30 separate websites. Hubbell wanted one master site where any product could be found, with each brand keeping its own site, built from a shared set of components.

Many of the brands were wary of the One Hubbell direction, and a few said so plainly, in the room, while we were doing the work. That kept up through discovery and the workshops. The whole project was at risk.

Approach

I led a team of two to three designers and ran workshops with 30+ people from across the brands, including senior VPs: personas first, then customer journeys, then a card sort with the full group. The card sort showed how hard their products were to organize. If it was that hard for the experts in the room, it would be harder still for their customers, and that was the case for doing research on the structure.

What settled the worry was showing everyone what it would actually look like: a set of templates and components that took on each brand's colors, with room to arrange content the way each brand needed. Once people understood that, everyone was on board.

Outcome

We shipped around 40 sites on the shared system, Hubbell.com included, each with its own branding intact. The sites still run on that work today, which I think says the most about how well it held up. We also recommended research to guide the product structure going forward. Hubbell may have taken that on, but I'd rolled off by then.

What I learned

The workshops gave everyone a shared language, and the card sort made the case for research, but neither one changed minds. People were worried about losing their brand, and talking about a system couldn't answer that. Seeing their own brand inside it did.

My role

I led the designers and ran the workshop program. My hands-on work was mostly wireframes, along with some design work alongside the team.
