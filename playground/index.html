Welcome.

This is where I keep experiments and things I make for myself. Most of it is half-built, and I like it that way. It's where I try new tools before I'd bring them to a team.

  1. Room 01In progress

    A mobile game

    A physics runner with zombies, for iPhone and Android, that I'm building completely on my own. I don't write code, so it's a fairly honest test of how far design skills and AI tools can take one person.

    I make the 3D models and artwork with a mix of modeling and AI tools, and I work through the code with AI coding assistants. Early on I settled on one-finger controls. The design doc is still a work in progress, and so is the game.

  2. Room 02Contest entry

    A 15-second commercial

    A drinks brand is running a contest for a 15-second Big Game commercial, and I'm entering on my own. I'm producing it mostly with AI video tools, maybe with some real footage mixed in.

    I wanted something a little subversive, so I started by developing three concepts before committing to one.

  3. Room 03Live

    This website

    I designed and built this portfolio with an AI coding tool. Along the way it picked up a few things a portfolio doesn't usually have: a private review mode, where invited reviewers leave comments only I can read, and a weekly analytics report that pulls itself together every Monday morning.

    It's also been good practice at something I ask of teams: put a real thing in front of people early and let their feedback shape it.

  4. Room 04Running

    A trading experiment

    A simulated stock portfolio, with no real money, that buys and sells on its own once a week for a few months. It started with $10,000, split between long-term holdings and a smaller portion it trades more actively. An AI model does the market research and makes the calls, and a dashboard tracks how it's doing.

    I'm not interested in day trading. What I want is the reasoning: why each stock was bought or sold, which trends it's following, and whether those calls hold up against history.
