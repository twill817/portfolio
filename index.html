Public sector2023–2024

Commonwealth of Pennsylvania

Residents had to know which agency owned their task before they could find it, the opposite of the governor's "No wrong door" commitment.

Associate Creative Director, Experience · Publicis Sapient

  • My part

    I owned the UX architecture and led four designers, and set direction for the visual design lead, a fellow associate creative director. We worked directly with the governor's digital team leads. In workshops with each agency, I reorganized their sites around how residents look for content and take it in. I also set the content strategy, replacing folder-based organization with tagging so search could filter the way residents think, including by life event.

  • What happened

    PA.gov launched in phases on that architecture, completing in January 2025. Agencies weren't required to adopt the new structure, and all but a handful did.

  • 62 sites
  • 43 agencies
  • 37% → 95% accessibility audit score
  • ~9M visits in December 2024

Grupo Coppel

Coppel's leadership wanted design work on par with the best tech companies, and set up a Digital Center of Excellence to get there. I joined as a founding member.

Associate Creative Director, Experience · Publicis Sapient

  • My part

    I ran a skills-gap assessment against that bar, then rewrote most of the design organization's job descriptions around quality and mentoring, with clear criteria for hiring and promotion. Industrial B2B · New product · AI6 months

Visualization tool

Contractors needed to show homeowners how real roofing and siding would look on their house, then turn that choice straight into an order.

Associate Creative Director, Experience · Publicis Sapient

  • My part

    I led one to three designers from first research to final sprint, designing the full flow from project setup and visualization through homeowner approval to the order. The team committed early to real-time material rendering, and it never looked convincing. Contractor research showed that quality mattered more than speed. I introduced Figma Make prototyping to the team and used it to make the case for generative-AI rendering. It also took a day or two off each sprint review.

  • The lesson

    I let that direction stay open too long. By the time the research and prototypes reached the client's decision-maker, much of the real-time build was done. The client held course, and the team added an AI render on top to recover quality. Next time I'll test the riskiest assumptions before the build commits.

  • Figma Make brought into the team
  • 1–2 days off each sprint review
  • Generative-AI render added

AI in my process

I use AI where it shortens the distance between an idea and something people can react to. The judgment calls still sit with me and the team.

  • 01

    Prototyping and catalog analysis

    On the visualization tool, I brought Figma Make into the team's process. It took a day or two off each sprint review and helped make the case for generative-AI rendering. We also used AI for a light analysis of the client's product catalog, which wasn't built with homeowners in mind, to find the data the homeowner experience was missing. Some of those changes were made.

  • 02

    Documentation at scale

    At Coppel, I used LLMs for much of the documentation and for rewriting the design organization's job descriptions. I set the criteria and did the final edits; the models handled the drafting.

  • 03

    Knowing when to set it aside

    On PA.gov, I tried LLMs on parts of the information architecture. At the time they invented too much and drifted from the direction I gave them, so I set them aside and did that work without them.

I also designed and built this site with Claude Code, including its private review mode for invited reviewers and a weekly analytics report.

How I work

Build shared ground before arguing structure

People who disagree about structure usually disagree about who they're serving. I get agreement on that first, then let the group reach the structure together.

Earn adoption instead of requiring it

Some of my most lasting work had no mandate behind it. I bring product and engineering in early, so a proposal reaches leadership with their support behind it. Agencies and teams adopted the work because it held up for them.

Make it testable

I prototype to put real designs in front of people early, before a team commits to a direction. Real reactions surface the risky assumptions while they're still cheap to change.
