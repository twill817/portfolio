## The problem

Meridian had tried to build a design system twice before I joined — both attempts stalled when the sponsoring team was reorganized and the system lost its owner and its budget. By the time I arrived, "design system" was a phrase that made engineering leads visibly tired.

The technical work of building a component library was the easy part. The real problem was organizational: how do you build something that needs sustained cross-team investment, inside a company that reorganizes every 12–18 months?

## My role

I proposed and led the third attempt as Principal UX Designer, reporting to the VP of Product Design, with a rotating pool of two to four engineers contributed part-time from different product teams rather than a single dedicated team.

## Process

**Fund it like infrastructure, not like a project.** I made the case to leadership to fund the design system out of a shared platform budget rather than any single product team's roadmap — so its survival didn't depend on one team's priorities surviving a reorg.

**Design for adoption, not mandate.** Rather than requiring teams to migrate, I made the system the fastest path to shipping a compliant, accessible UI — pre-built patterns for the most common (and most audited) flows, like consent forms and scheduling. Teams adopted it because it was less work, not because they were told to.

**Document ownership, not just components.** Every component in the library has a named directly responsible individual and a listed backup, reviewed quarterly — so "who owns this" survives any single reorg.

## Outcome

The design system has now survived three organizational restructures intact, including one that eliminated the original sponsoring team entirely. Adoption grew from 2 product teams at launch to 11 within two years. It remains the backbone referenced throughout the Meridian platform work above.
