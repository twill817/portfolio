## The problem

Kestrel's flagship product had grown organically for over a decade. Every team had shipped its own patterns, its own modals, its own definition of a "primary button." Customers weren't complaining about looks — they were complaining about time. Tasks that should take two minutes took ten, because nothing behaved consistently from one screen to the next.

The business constraint was as important as the design one: this product ran factory floors and logistics operations for customers who couldn't tolerate a broken workflow, even for a day. A conventional "redesign and relaunch" was not an option.

## My role

I was the senior product designer on a three-person design team, embedded directly with two engineering pods. I owned the interaction design and design system architecture; I partnered closely with a researcher and a second designer who focused on the reporting module.

## Process

**Audit first.** Before proposing anything new, I catalogued every UI pattern in active use — 47 button styles, 12 different date pickers, 6 unrelated color palettes. That audit became the business case for a design system, translated into engineering hours saved per pattern consolidated.

**Design in place.** Rather than a big-bang redesign, we shipped the new design system component-by-component, behind flags, into the existing product. Power users — the ones most resistant to change — got early access and direct lines to me for feedback.

**Protect the muscle memory.** For our highest-frequency workflows, we ran side-by-side usability sessions comparing old and new, timing task completion, before any change shipped broadly. If the new pattern wasn't measurably faster, it didn't ship as-is.

## Outcome

Over eighteen months, the design system rolled out across the full product with zero forced-migration downtime. Support tickets related to UI confusion dropped 34%. New engineer onboarding time to ship a UI change dropped from roughly three weeks to four days, because the component library did the hard decisions for them.

The design system I built the foundations for during this project is still, in modified form, in production over a decade later.
