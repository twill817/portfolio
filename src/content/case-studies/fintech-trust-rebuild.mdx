## The problem

Six months before I joined, Northline shipped a full redesign of its mobile banking app. It looked modern. It also buried the "report a fraudulent charge" flow three taps deeper, changed the transfer confirmation pattern without warning, and triggered a wave of one-star reviews and a measurable spike in call-center volume. Trust, for a bank, is the entire product — and they had spent it carelessly.

I was hired to lead design through the recovery, and to make sure it couldn't happen again.

## My role

As Head of Design, I led a team of twelve designers and researchers across the consumer banking, small-business, and internal-tools product lines. For this project specifically, I was the hands-on design lead working directly with the mobile engineering team and the VP of Product.

## Process

**Triage by risk, not by opinion.** Instead of debating aesthetics, we scored every changed flow by financial and safety risk. Fraud reporting, transfers, and account recovery went to the top of the list regardless of how much design effort each required.

**Bring in the people who complained.** We recruited directly from the pool of customers who had left negative reviews or called support. Watching a longtime customer hesitate before tapping "confirm transfer" — afraid it wouldn't work the way it used to — reframed the whole team's sense of urgency.

**Change the process, not just the product.** I introduced a mandatory risk-and-reversibility review for any change touching money-movement or account-security flows, with sign-off required from design, engineering, and compliance before release. This was the more durable fix — the redesign was a symptom of a process that let a risky change ship without enough scrutiny.

## Outcome

Within four months, App Store rating recovered from 2.1 to 4.3 stars. Fraud-reporting completion time dropped by 40%. Call-center volume tied to "how do I..." questions fell by a quarter. The risk-review process I introduced is still part of Northline's release checklist today.
