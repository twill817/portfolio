How I workA short note

Earning adoption without the authority to require it

Some of my most lasting work happened where I didn't have the authority to demand anything.

At Hubbell, the brands ran as their own businesses, and nobody could order them onto a shared system. On PA.gov, agencies could have opted out of the new structure. At Coppel, I was advising a design organization I didn't run. In each case people had to choose the work, so I've had to learn what gets them to.

Hubbell

Show them their own thing inside it.

A few brands said plainly, in the room, that they didn't want One Hubbell. We ran workshops with 30+ people and a card sort, and those helped, but neither changed minds. What did was each brand seeing its own colors and content inside the shared templates. They were worried about losing their brand, and talking about a system couldn't answer that. About 40 sites shipped on it, and they still run on that work.

PA.gov

Make the right way the easy way.

The people running agency sites were mostly site managers, not designers or writers. We'd planned to organize around life events, but no one on the client side would own that content. So we gave agencies a simpler pattern, examples, and a guidelines site with training. Once they had the pattern, they didn't have to think about it, and all but a handful came along.

Grupo Coppel

Let the people who'll live with it write it.

I didn't hand the design team its principles. I ran a workshop and they wrote their own five. They're still in Coppel's design system.

When it doesn't work

It doesn't always work. At Coppel I also brought leadership a new team structure, with product and technology already behind it. They agreed, then put it on hold behind a CEO-led redesign. I think that's the limit: support can get an idea accepted, but it can't decide when it happens.
